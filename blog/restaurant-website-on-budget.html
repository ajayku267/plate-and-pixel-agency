    Your Options

    There are three realistic ways to get a restaurant website on a limited budget:

    Option 1: Website Builder (Wix, Squarespace, etc.)

    Website builders let you build a site yourself using drag-and-drop tools. The initial cost is low — some plans start free or at a few hundred rupees per month. However: monthly fees add up (₹500–₹2,000+ per month), the templates are generic, performance is often slower than custom-built sites, and you have to do all the work yourself including updates.

    Best for: Someone with time and basic technical confidence who wants full hands-on control.

    Option 2: Professional Agency Starter Package

    Agencies that specialise in restaurant websites — like Plates & Pixels — offer starter packages specifically designed for small restaurants and cafés. These are professionally designed, mobile-first, and include all the essentials: menu, gallery, WhatsApp, location, SEO basics.

    At ₹3,999 as a one-time payment, this often works out cheaper than a website builder over 12–18 months — and without requiring any ongoing management on your part.

    Best for: Restaurant owners who want a proper professional result without managing it themselves.

    Option 3: Freelancer

    Individual freelancers can build websites at varying price points. Quality varies significantly. The risk is that a very cheap freelancer may produce a result that looks amateurish or is poorly optimised — which can do more harm than good.

    Best for: When you can personally vet the freelancer's portfolio and have seen quality they've produced for similar restaurants.

    What to Prioritise on a Budget

    If budget is a real constraint, focus on these essentials first:

    1. Mobile-friendly design — non-negotiable
    2. Online menu — the single most important page
    3. Address and opening hours — clearly visible
    4. WhatsApp contact button — the easiest contact method
    5. At least one real food photo — real is always better than stock

    These five elements, done well, cover the vast majority of what a restaurant website needs to be effective. You can add more pages, more features, and more content later as your business grows.

    Where You Can Save Money

    • Photos — shoot on your own smartphone in good natural light rather than hiring a photographer initially
    • Content — write your own menu descriptions and about section rather than paying for copywriting
    • Domain — a .in domain is typically cheaper than a .com domain and equally valid for an Indian restaurant
    • Hosting — static websites (like those we build) can often be hosted free or at very low cost on platforms like Netlify

    Think About Long-Term Value

    A ₹3,999 website that brings in two additional bookings per month pays for itself in the first month at most restaurants. A website isn't a cost — it's an investment that compounds over time.

    Compare it to what you might spend on Swiggy or Zomato commission in a single week. A properly built restaurant website works for years with minimal ongoing cost — no commission, no monthly subscription, no platform dependency.
