7 Things Every Restaurant Website Needs

📅 August 2026 🕒 7 min read Plates & Pixels

A restaurant website isn't just a digital version of your printed menu. Done well, it actively works to bring customers through your door — by showing up in Google searches, making a strong first impression, and making it effortless to find you, contact you, and decide to visit.

Done poorly — or not done at all — it leaves you invisible to anyone who searches online. This article covers the seven features that separate a restaurant website that actually works from one that just sits on the internet.

    1. Mobile-Friendly Design

    Most of your potential customers will first visit your website on a smartphone — often while they're already out, deciding where to eat. If your website doesn't display well on a phone, they'll leave within seconds and move on to a restaurant whose site does work.

    Mobile-friendly means the text is readable without zooming, buttons are easy to tap, images load quickly, and the menu is easy to navigate. It's not about having a separate mobile site — it's about one design that adapts to any screen size.

    Google also uses the mobile version of your site to determine search rankings. A site that works poorly on mobile will rank lower than a mobile-optimised one.

    2. An Easy-to-Use Online Menu

    This is non-negotiable. The menu is the single most important page on a restaurant website. Customers decide where to eat based on what's on offer — they need to be able to browse your menu quickly and easily on their phone.

    Your online menu should be a proper web page — not a PDF, not a photo. It should be organised into clear categories, show prices, and be readable without zooming. Food photos alongside each item increase appetite and the chance of conversion.

    A proper HTML menu page also helps with SEO: Google can read every dish and ingredient, making it possible for your restaurant to appear in searches for specific cuisine types and dishes.

    3. High-Quality Food Photography

    People eat with their eyes first. A gallery of genuine, well-lit photos of your food and your space is one of the most powerful conversion tools on your website. It answers the question every new customer is asking: "Does this place look worth visiting?"

    You don't necessarily need a professional photographer — many modern smartphones take excellent food photos in good natural lighting. What matters is authenticity: real food, real space, genuinely presented. Stock food photos create distrust the moment a customer visits and the food looks different.

    4. WhatsApp and Contact Options

    For restaurants and cafés across India, WhatsApp is how people communicate. A visible WhatsApp button on your website — ideally floating on every page — gives customers an instant, frictionless way to reach you. Reservations, group bookings, takeaway orders, and simple questions can all be handled via WhatsApp.

    In addition to WhatsApp, your website should display a clickable phone number and ideally a contact form or email address. The goal is to make it as easy as possible for an interested customer to get in touch.

    5. Google Maps and Location

    A restaurant website must tell customers exactly where you are. This means a clear address displayed in text (so Google can read it), an embedded Google Maps element, and ideally some additional context — nearby landmarks, parking information, or public transport options.

    Your address should appear on your homepage and on a dedicated contact page. When your address is clearly visible and consistent across your website and Google Business Profile, Google is much more likely to show you in local search results and on Google Maps.

    6. Reservations and Ordering

    How a customer contacts you to reserve a table or place an order depends on your restaurant type. You have several options:

    • WhatsApp button — ideal for casual restaurants, cafés, and takeaway operations
    • Phone number — simple and universal, works for any restaurant
    • Online reservation form — more formal, suitable for higher-demand restaurants
    • Third-party booking integration — platforms like Dineout or EazyDiner for fine dining

    For most small-to-medium restaurants, a prominent WhatsApp button combined with a clear phone number is the most practical and cost-effective approach. It requires no monthly subscription, no commission, and no setup complexity.

    7. Fast Loading Speed

    A restaurant website that takes more than 3–4 seconds to load on a mobile connection will lose a significant portion of its visitors. People searching for somewhere to eat on their phone are often impatient — if your site is slow, they'll go elsewhere.

    Fast loading also matters for Google rankings. Pages that load quickly provide a better user experience, and Google rewards that with better search visibility.

    Fast loading comes from proper image optimisation (compressing images without sacrificing quality), clean code, and choosing a reliable hosting provider. A well-built restaurant website should load within 2–3 seconds on a typical mobile connection.

    The complete checklist: Mobile-friendly design ✓ Online menu ✓ Food photography ✓ WhatsApp contact ✓ Google Maps ✓ Reservation options ✓ Fast loading ✓

    Frequently Asked Questions

    What is the most important feature on a restaurant website?+

    An online menu is the most important element. Customers want to know what you serve before deciding to visit. A well-presented, mobile-friendly menu can make the difference between a visitor booking a table and moving on to a competitor.

    Do restaurant websites need to be mobile-friendly?+

    Absolutely. Most customers browse on smartphones. A website that doesn't work well on mobile loses visitors and ranks lower in Google search results. Mobile-friendly design is not optional.

    Should a restaurant website have a WhatsApp button?+

    Yes, especially for restaurants in India where WhatsApp is the primary communication channel. A WhatsApp button makes it effortless for customers to ask questions, make reservations, or place orders directly.

    How many pages should a restaurant website have?+

    Most restaurants do well with 3–5 pages: home, menu, gallery, about, and contact. Start with the essentials and expand based on what your customers actually need.
