What Should a Restaurant Website Include? The Complete Checklist

📅 August 2026 🕒 8 min read Plates & Pixels

If you're getting a restaurant website built — or reviewing your existing one — it helps to know exactly what should be on it. Not every restaurant needs the same features, but there are certain elements that every restaurant website should include to properly serve its visitors and support the business.

This guide covers the essential sections, features, and content that belong on a restaurant website in 2026 — and what each element actually does for your business.

    1. A Strong Homepage

    Your homepage is the first impression most visitors get of your restaurant. It should communicate three things immediately: what you are, where you are, and what makes you worth visiting.

    A well-designed restaurant homepage typically includes:

    • A clear headline — your restaurant name and what you serve
    • A high-quality hero image or short video of your food or interior
    • A brief description — the type of cuisine, your location, and your atmosphere
    • A prominent call to action — "View Menu," "Book a Table," or "Order via WhatsApp"
    • Your opening hours and address, visible without scrolling

    The homepage doesn't need to contain everything. Its job is to make a great first impression and direct visitors to the information they're looking for.

    2. An Online Menu

    This is the most important page on your restaurant website. Customers want to know what you serve before they decide to visit, and an online menu is how you answer that question.

    Your online menu should:

    • Be a proper web page — not a PDF or an Instagram photo
    • Be organised into categories (starters, mains, desserts, drinks)
    • Include item names, descriptions, and prices
    • Include food photos where possible
    • Highlight dietary options — vegetarian, vegan, gluten-free
    • Be easy to read on a mobile phone without zooming
    • Be kept up to date when items or prices change

    A properly built menu page also helps with restaurant SEO. Google can read every item on your menu, which means your restaurant can appear in searches for specific dishes and cuisine types.

    3. A Photo Gallery

    Food is visual. Customers decide where to eat based on how the food looks — and a gallery of high-quality photos on your website is one of the most effective ways to attract new visitors.

    Your gallery should include:

    • Food photos — your best dishes, well-lit and honestly presented
    • Interior photos — your dining space, atmosphere, and décor
    • Exterior photos — your entrance and signage
    • Team photos where relevant — for restaurants with a chef-forward identity

    Good photos don't necessarily require a professional photographer. Many modern smartphones can take excellent food photos in good natural light. What matters is authenticity — real food, real space, no stock images.

    4. Location and Opening Hours

    One of the most common things people look for on a restaurant website is the address and hours. Make this information easy to find — ideally visible on the homepage and repeated on the contact page.

    Include:

    • Your full address with a Google Maps embed
    • Opening hours for each day of the week
    • Any special holiday hours
    • Parking information if relevant

    This information also feeds into Google search results. When your address and hours are clearly displayed on your website, Google is more likely to show them accurately in search results and on Google Maps.

    5. Contact Options

    Make it as easy as possible for potential customers to reach you. A restaurant website should include multiple contact options:

    • WhatsApp button — for direct, instant messaging. Particularly valuable for reservations and takeaway orders.
    • Phone number — clearly clickable on mobile
    • Email address — for corporate enquiries, events, and group bookings
    • Contact form — for structured enquiries
    • Instagram link — for social proof and content

    6. An About Section

    The About section is often underestimated. For local restaurants, it's a chance to build a genuine connection with potential customers — to tell the story of who you are, why you started, what you believe in, and what makes your restaurant different.

    This doesn't need to be long. A few honest, well-written paragraphs about your restaurant's identity are far more effective than generic marketing language. If you have a chef with a specific background, a farm-to-table philosophy, or a family story behind the restaurant, share it here.

    7. SEO Elements

    A restaurant website that nobody can find is not doing its job. Every page of your website should include basic SEO elements:

    • A descriptive page title including your restaurant name and location
    • A meta description summarising the page
    • Proper heading structure (one H1 per page, followed by H2 and H3 subheadings)
    • Your address mentioned in text — not just embedded in a map
    • Fast loading speed and mobile-friendly design

    8. Mobile-First Design

    Most people who visit your restaurant website will do so on a smartphone. This means the mobile version of your site needs to be excellent — not just functional. Text should be readable without zooming, buttons should be easy to tap, images should load quickly, and the menu should be easy to navigate.

    Google also primarily uses the mobile version of your website to determine how to rank it. A website that works poorly on mobile will rank lower than one that is mobile-optimised.

    9. A Prominent WhatsApp Button

    For restaurants and cafés across India, WhatsApp is the primary channel for instant communication. A floating WhatsApp button on your website — visible on every page — makes it effortless for customers to contact you, ask questions, make reservations, or place orders.

    The message can be pre-filled with context, such as "Hi, I'd like to reserve a table" — making the enquiry even faster for the customer and more useful for you.

    Frequently Asked Questions

    What pages should a restaurant website have?+

    At minimum: homepage, menu, gallery, about, and contact. Larger restaurants may add event pages, reservation systems, or blog content. Start with the essentials and expand as needed.

    Should my restaurant website have a booking system?+

    It depends on your restaurant type. For high-demand venues, a booking system makes sense. For casual cafés and smaller restaurants, a WhatsApp contact button or phone number often works just as well and avoids the complexity and cost of booking software.

    Do I need professional photos for my restaurant website?+

    Good quality photos make a real difference, but professional photography isn't always necessary. High-quality smartphone photos taken in good lighting can work well. The key is authentic, well-lit images of your actual food and space — not stock photos.

    Should my restaurant website include prices?+

    Yes, where practical. Customers appreciate price transparency and it helps set expectations before they visit. If prices change frequently, make sure to keep the menu updated — an outdated price on your website can cause frustration.
